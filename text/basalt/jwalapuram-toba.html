The Toba ash at Jwalapuram

c. 74,000 years ago · Event · Jwalapuram, Kurnool district, Andhra Pradesh

Ash from the greatest eruption of the last two million years, at Toba in Sumatra, blankets a Deccan valley – with stone tools below the ash and above it, at the centre of a fierce argument.

About 74,000 years ago the Toba caldera in Sumatra produced the largest volcanic eruption of the Quaternary, and its ash fell across the Indian peninsula in a layer that still survives in valley fills from the Son to the Jurreru. At Jwalapuram in the Kurnool district, excavations reported from 2007 found the ash bed within a long archaeological sequence: Middle Palaeolithic stone tools beneath it and, crucially, similar tools above it – evidence read by the excavators as human populations in the Deccan surviving the eruption’s aftermath more or less in place.

The claim mattered because Toba had been cast as a global catastrophe – the ‘volcanic winter’ once invoked to explain an apparent bottleneck in human populations – and because which humans made the Jwalapuram tools bears on when modern people first reached India: before Toba, on the excavators’ reading, or after, as many geneticists’ chronologies prefer. Both the bottleneck and the pre-Toba presence have been strongly contested, and the debate – archaeology against genetics, with the ash as the timestamp – continues in the literature. What is not contested is the marker itself: a matter of days of Sumatran geology, fixed in Deccan stratigraphy, against which everything nearby can be dated.

The Kurnool country compounds the record: its limestone caves, dug since the 1880s, hold the region’s later Palaeolithic and its bone tools. Between ash, caves and river gravels, this corner of the plateau is where the Deccan’s human deep time is argued out.

In the story

A foreign volcano supplies the Deccan’s best clock, and the plateau becomes evidence in the debate over our own species’ spread. It is this collection’s version of a disputed chronicle: one layer, two schools, and the argument itself part of the story.
