Water between the flows

aquifers since the Traps; wells since antiquity · Process · Ahmadnagar district, Maharashtra

Unfractured basalt has very low permeability; the Deccan’s wells live in the pile’s openings – weathered mantles, joints and fractures, vesicular flow tops and the horizons between flow and flow. Where a well works is the lava pile’s anatomy, read from below.

Unfractured massive basalt has very low permeability: a well sunk into an unbroken flow interior finds rock, not water – though where jointing and fracturing open it, even the massive flows can yield. The groundwater of the basalt Deccan lives instead in the pile’s interruptions – the weathered mantle near the surface, the networks of cooling joints and fractures, the vesicular and brecciated tops of individual flows, and the interflow beds between eruptions, with interflow contacts that may host aquifers while the clay-rich red bole itself commonly acts as an aquitard or confining layer. The result, as Maharashtra’s groundwater surveys and the hydrogeological literature describe it, is not one aquifer but a stack of poor ones: thin, discontinuous, quick to fill in the monsoon and quick to fall through the dry months, with each flow contact capable of perching its own modest water table. Whether a village well succeeds is decided by which of these horizons it happens to strike – the eruptive anatomy of period four, read from below.

The historic Deccan lived on this stack. The dug wells of the timeline’s countryside – the leather-bucket lifts of the revenue records, the garden crops of the settlement surveys – drew from the shallow weathered and jointed zone, a resource generous enough in good years to make bagayat, watered land, the most valued category in every assessment, and shallow enough to fail in the years the monsoon entry describes; in drought, the rapid depletion of these shallow horizons compounds the failure of the rains. The modern sequel is the borewell, chasing deeper fractures with compressors, and the same anatomy still rules: the drillers’ luck is stratigraphy.

Groundwater is thus the Traps’ daily gift, as regur is its agricultural one – the eruption of period four and the weathering of period five converted, by nothing more than gravity and the monsoon, into the water that decided where the other timeline’s villages could stand. Of all this collection’s entries, this is the one the plateau’s inhabitants consult most often, if unknowingly, every time a well is sunk.

In the story

The entry closes the circuit between the lava pile and daily life: the same flow-by-flow architecture that the Traps entry describes and the cave-cutters exploited also decides where water waits, and so where the Deccan timeline’s villages, gardens and famines fell. Basalt gives the plateau its soil above and its water below – and rations both.
