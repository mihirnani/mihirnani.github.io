Coda: the argument about the Traps

1980 – present · Document · The literature

Impact against volcano, plume against plate: since 1980 the Deccan has been a proving ground for how the earth sciences argue – and the plateau’s deep history is still being revised beneath it.

This collection’s fourth period is not settled science but a live case file. Since the Alvarez impact hypothesis of 1980 and Courtillot’s volcanic counter-case, the Deccan has sat at the centre of the earth sciences’ most public argument – how the Cretaceous ended – and the argument has driven the plateau’s geology to extraordinary precision: flow-by-flow chemostratigraphy up the Ghats, argon and uranium–lead dates disagreeing by mere tens of thousands of years, models of volcanic gas against impact winter, and the 2015 proposal that the asteroid itself accelerated the eruptions. In parallel runs the quieter dispute over the plume: whether a deep mantle column fed the Traps and the Réunion chain, or whether plate-scale cracking suffices – a debate about how the planet’s interior works, argued largely on Deccan evidence.

The revisions reach the whole collection. The Traps’ volume, duration and pulses; the timing of India’s partings and its sprint; the age of the escarpment’s retreat and of the laterites; the meaning of the Toba ash – every period here carries error bars, and several will read differently in twenty years. That is not a weakness to apologise for but the condition of the subject: like the other timeline’s contested dates, marked and argued, the deep history is a working document.

The coda, then, mirrors the Deccan timeline’s: that collection ends with the first historians of a lost world; this one ends with the geologists and geophysicists still writing theirs – the plateau’s oldest history being its least finished.

In the story

The collection closes by admitting its own provisionality: the Deccan’s deep past is an active frontier, and the entries here are the current state of an argument, not its verdict. In this site’s terms, the rock is still being read.
