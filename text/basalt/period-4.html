Period 04 · c. 67–65 Ma

The Traps

The event itself: a million cubic kilometres of basalt in less than a million years, the step landscape of the western Deccan, and a share – still argued over – in the death of the dinosaurs.

Around 66 million years ago the Deccan was resurfaced. Flood basalts – fed, in the standard model, by the Réunion plume – buried the west of the peninsula under lava piles that at Mahabaleshwar still stand more than a kilometre thick, flow upon flow, the stepped profile that gave the Traps their Scandinavian name. The eruptions bracket the boundary at which the dinosaurs died, and the Deccan's share in that extinction – against the asteroid at Chicxulub – is one of the liveliest arguments in earth science, pursued with precisely dated lava flows the way a historian pursues a chronicle. Between the flows, in the quiet intervals, lakes and soils formed and preserved the frogs, molluscs and plants of the intertrappean beds: the Deccan's own fossil archive of the world the eruptions ended. Everything the later collections describe – the forts, the soils, the scarps – happens on this basalt or beside it.

  • 66.0 Ma The Traps and the death of the dinosaurs

    The eruptions bracket the extinction that ended the Mesozoic; whether the Deccan shared the killing with the Chicxulub asteroid is one of science’s longest-running trials.

  • c. 66 Ma – present The Réunion plume

    In the standard model, the mantle upwelling that fed the Traps did not die with them: as India moved on, the plume’s later work built the Laccadives, the Maldives and Mauritius, and it erupts today at Réunion.
