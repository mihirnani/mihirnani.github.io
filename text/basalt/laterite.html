Laterite

Cenozoic; named 1807 · Formation · Angadipuram, Kerala

Rain and time rot rock into a rusty crust that cuts soft and hardens in air; Francis Buchanan named it at Angadipuram in 1807, from the Latin for brick.

Where a wet–dry tropical climate works on a stable surface long enough, it destroys the rock’s original chemistry: silica and bases wash away, iron and aluminium remain, and the result is laterite – a rusty, pitted, vesicular crust, metres to tens of metres thick, capping plateaus along the Ghats, the Konkan and the western Deccan’s flat summits. It is duricrust rather than bedrock: a climate’s signature, written over whatever lay beneath, basalt and gneiss alike, and much of it formed on land surfaces of great age – the high-level laterites of the Ghats cap some of the oldest ground in the peninsula.

The name is a Deccan export. On his survey of 1800–01, published in 1807, Francis Buchanan – the same Company surgeon-surveyor whose Mysore journey supplies sources to the Deccan timeline – described at Angadipuram in Malabar a material soft enough to cut with a spade that hardened irreversibly in the sun, and named it laterite, from later, brick. The property he noticed built a civilisation’s worth of walls: laterite blocks harden into weatherproof masonry, and from the Konkan forts to the gateways of Bidar – whose fort in the Deccan timeline is largely laterite on its laterite plateau – the crust has been quarried as self-firing brick. A pillar at Angadipuram now marks the type locality.

Laterite is also an obituary for landscapes: each sheet records a surface that stood stable under tropical weathering for millions of years before uplift or incision stranded it. The Deccan’s stepped skyline of capped plateaus is, in this reading, a stack of retired ground levels – the plateau remembering its own former surfaces.

In the story

Half the collection’s title, and the bridge material between its rocks and its people: a crust the climate made, a Company surveyor named, and the Deccan timeline’s Bidar built with. Basalt is the Deccan’s body; laterite is its skin.
