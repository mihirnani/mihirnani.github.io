Period 01 · c. 3,600–2,500 Ma

The Basement

The oldest Deccan: grey gneisses, greenstone belts and a final flood of granite weld the southern craton together.

Everything in this collection rests on the basement. The southern half of the peninsula is one of the world's old continental nuclei, a craton whose grey gneisses were already ancient when most of the earth's present crust had yet to form. Between about 3,600 and 2,500 million years ago the pieces assembled: the banded Peninsular Gneiss, the greenstone belts of the Dharwar country with their gold, and, at the end, the long red line of the Closepet Granite marking the craton's consolidation. When this period closes, the land that will one day carry Vijayanagara, Golconda and the cotton fields is finished as crust; nearly everything since has been cover, decoration and wear. The evidence is read from the rocks themselves, by radiometric dating and field mapping, and the dates carry uncertainties of tens of millions of years – a precision that would embarrass a historian and delights a geologist.

  • c. 3,400–2,500 Ma The Peninsular Gneiss

    The grey banded gneisses that floor the southern peninsula are among the oldest rocks in India; the hill in Bengaluru's Lalbagh garden is their classic exposure, protected as a national geological monument.

  • c. 2,900–2,600 Ma The Dharwar greenstones

    Belts of altered lavas and sediments folded into the gneiss country – the remains of Archaean sea floors, and the source of the craton's iron, manganese and gold.

    Formation · The old craton

  • c. 2,550 Ma; mined to AD 2001 Kolar and the old gold

    Gold-bearing quartz veins in a greenstone belt east of Bengaluru, worked in antiquity, reopened under the Raj, and mined to depths of three kilometres before closing in 2001.

    Place · The old craton

  • c. 2,500 Ma The Closepet Granite

    A four-hundred-kilometre ribbon of pink granite marking the craton's final welding – and, weathered into domes and boulder piles, the theatrical scenery of the southern Deccan.
