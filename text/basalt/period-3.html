Period 03 · c. 250–67 Ma

The Breakup

Gondwana comes apart, the coasts are made, and India casts off for its sprint north with dinosaurs aboard.

The peninsula’s outline is a tearing pattern. From about 180 million years ago Gondwana came apart in stages, and each parting made a piece of the Indian margin: Africa first, then Antarctica and Australia, then – about 88 million years ago – Madagascar, whose departure made the Konkan edge and set the line the Western Ghats would follow. What remained was an island continent moving north at among the fastest sustained speeds known for a continental plate, carrying a cargo of titanosaurs whose bones and eggshells survive in the Lameta beds along the plateau’s northern rim. The period ends with India over a hot spot in the mantle, the fuse already burning under the greatest event in the Deccan’s history.

  • c. 180–88 Ma The breakup of Gondwana

    The supercontinent tore apart in stages, and each tearing made a piece of India’s outline; the parting from Madagascar, about 88 million years ago, drew the west coast the Ghats now stand behind.

  • c. 70–66 Ma Dinosaurs at the edge of the Traps

    The Lameta beds along the plateau’s northern rim preserve India’s last dinosaurs – titanosaurs and the predator Rajasaurus, with nesting grounds of fossil eggs – sealed beneath the first lava flows.
