The stones of the forts

c. AD 1100–1700 · Place · Daulatabad, Maharashtra

Every fortress in the Deccan timeline is a geology lesson: trap scarps at Daulatabad and the Maratha strongholds, granite at Golconda and Hampi, laterite at Bidar – the dynasties built with whatever period of this collection they stood on.

Read with this collection in hand, the Deccan timeline's forts sort themselves by bedrock. Daulatabad is a Trap outlier scarped into a vertical face of some fifty to sixty-five metres, with a rock-cut moat sunk further into the basalt below – the pile of period four, edited; the Maratha forts of the western entries, Torna to Raigad, are mesa-tops of the same lavas, their walls often no more than a parapet on a natural cliff. Golconda and Hampi are period one: granite boulder hills whose weathered domes and wedged boulders were incorporated whole into the fortification, masonry and craton interleaved. Bidar is period five's crust, a laterite plateau quarried into its own ramparts, the ditch cut soft and the blocks hardening in the sun exactly as Buchanan later described. Even the gun-founding of the sultanate entries leans on geology – the great cast pieces at Bijapur sit on towers of basalt rubble.

The sorting was not aesthetic. Each rock offered a different defensive geometry – the trap scarp's horizontal cliffs, the granite hill's chaos of covered approaches, the laterite plateau's diggable ditch – and siegecraft in the other timeline can be read as the art of arguing with these geometries: mining works where rock is soft, escalade where scarps are stepped, blockade where nothing else serves. When Aurangzeb's engineers sat before Golconda for eight months, the granite of the Archaean craton was an unlisted member of the garrison.

The building stones travelled, too: the dressed granite of Vijayanagara's temples, the basalt of the sultanate tombs, the laterite of the Konkan forts all moved by the ox-loads that the timeline's economies counted. Architecture in the Deccan is the local column of this collection, load-bearing.

In the story

The collection's most direct handshake with the Deccan timeline: its forts re-catalogued by period of rock. Sovereignty in the other collection is layered; here the layers are literal, and each dynasty defended the stratum it happened to hold.

In the Deccan timeline

Tughluq moves the capital to Daulatabad (1327) · Bidar (c. 1430–1619) · Vijayanagara, the city (c. 1500) · Aurangzeb takes Bijapur and Golconda (1686–1687)
