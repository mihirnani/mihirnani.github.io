Period 06 · 1799–1831

Paramountcy

Subsidiary alliances, two Maratha wars, a survey and a settlement: the plural Deccan becomes a hierarchy under Company paramountcy – administered districts beside subordinate princely states.

Wellesley’s system of subsidiary alliances turned the Nizam and the restored raja of Mysore into dependants, and the Treaty of Bassein did the same to the Peshwa. Two wars against the Marathas, at Assaye in 1803 and across the Deccan in 1817–18, ended the Peshwa’s sovereignty and the last independent Maratha challenge in the Deccan. What followed was administration: Lambton’s triangulation, Mackenzie’s manuscripts, Elphinstone’s settlement of the conquered territories, the cantonment at Pune, the Residency at Hyderabad and, in 1831, the Company’s commission in Mysore. By 1831 the Deccan was a set of districts and princely states on a Company map. What the map did not show was everything the Company had inherited beneath it – the grants, offices and hereditary rights of five centuries – and what to do with them is the subject of the last period.

  • 1799–1821 Colin Mackenzie’s collection

    A survey officer and his Indian assistants gathered manuscripts, inscriptions, drawings and coins across the southern Deccan on a scale never attempted before.

    Document · East India Company · Mysore · Other

  • 30 June 1799 Mysore restored

    Eight weeks after Tipu’s death the Company installed a five-year-old Wodeyar on a reduced Mysore throne, with Tipu’s own minister Purnaiya to run it.

    Event · Mysore · East India Company · Hyderabad

  • 10 April 1802 Lambton and the Great Trigonometrical Survey

    A baseline measured near Madras began the triangulation that would carry a web of measured angles across the peninsula and, in time, the subcontinent.

    Institution · East India Company

  • 25 October – 31 December 1802 Poona and the Treaty of Bassein

    Holkar took Pune and the Peshwa fled to the Company; the treaty signed at Bassein bought his restoration at the price of a subsidiary force, and the confederacy went to war over it.

    Treaty · Marathas · East India Company

  • 23 September 1803 Assaye, 1803

    Arthur Wellesley’s small army broke the trained battalions of Scindia and Bhonsle on the Kaitna, at a cost he remembered all his life.

    Battle · Marathas · East India Company · Hyderabad

  • 1803–1820s The Hyderabad Residency

    A Palladian mansion on the Musi housed the Company’s agent at the Nizam’s court, and the banking house next to it showed how power there was actually exercised.

    Place · Hyderabad · East India Company

  • 1810–1824 Palmer and Company

    A Hyderabad banking house with an Anglo-Indian name lent to the Nizam at twenty-four per cent under a Governor-General’s licence; when the Resident audited it, the Company became the Nizam’s creditor itself.

    Institution · Hyderabad · East India Company

  • 1817–1818 The Pindaris and the third Anglo-Maratha war

    A campaign against freebooting horsemen became the war that ended the Peshwa’s government, fought at Khadki, Koregaon and Ashti.

    Battle · Marathas · East India Company

  • 1818–1826 The Deccan Commission

    For eight years the Peshwa’s old dominions were governed by a Commissioner and a handful of collectors before being folded into the Bombay Presidency.

    Institution · East India Company

  • 3 June 1818 The Peshwa surrenders

    Bajirao II gave himself up to John Malcolm, accepted a pension and left the Deccan for a village on the Ganges, and the office of Peshwa was abolished.

    Event · Marathas · East India Company

  • 1818–1830s Pune under the Company

    The Peshwa’s capital became a cantonment town and a district headquarters of the Bombay Presidency, its palace a burnt shell.

    Place · East India Company · Marathas

  • 1818–1848 The Satara raj

    The Company freed Shivaji’s descendant from the Peshwa’s custody and made him a small treaty raja, then deposed his line within a generation.

    Institution · Marathas · East India Company

  • 25 October 1819 Elphinstone’s Report

    The Commissioner of the Deccan described the Peshwa’s government as he found it and argued for keeping much of it, for the moment, intact.

    Document · East India Company · Marathas

  • 1 January 1827 The Elphinstone Code

    The Bombay Regulations of 1827 put the courts, the police, the revenue and the ‘usage of the country’ into one code, usually described as the first systematic code in British India, and wrote the panchayat and the village officer into Company law.

    Document · East India Company

  • 1830–1831 The Nagar revolt and the Mysore Commission

    A peasant rising in the Malnad gave the Company its reason to invoke the treaty of 1799 and take over the government of Mysore for fifty years.

    Event · Mysore · East India Company
