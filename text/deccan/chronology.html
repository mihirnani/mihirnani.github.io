Chronology

The Deccan, year by year

Every dated line in the collection, including context events that have no entry of their own; where the collection has an entry for a line, it is linked at the end of it.

Backdrop – Delhi comes south 1296–1336

  • 1296Alauddin Khalji raids Devagiri, the Yadava capital; tribute imposed.
  • 1307/08–1311Malik Kafur’s campaigns: Devagiri reduced (1307/08), Warangal (1309–10), then Dwarasamudra and Madurai (1310–11).
  • 1318Harapaladeva executed; the Yadava state extinguished, Devagiri a Delhi province.
  • 1323Ulugh Khan, the future Muhammad bin Tughluq, takes Warangal; the Kakatiya kingdom ends.
  • 1327Tughluq moves the capital to Daulatabad. Entry →
  • c. 1327–1336Kampili falls and its refugee officers – by tradition including Harihara and Bukka – are dispersed; Delhi’s southern provinces begin to slip.
  • c. 1335Madurai breaks away from Delhi as an independent sultanate. Entry →

Two Foundations c. 1330–1500

  • c. 1325–1475Reddi and Velama kingdoms rule coastal Andhra and Telangana between the greater powers. Entry →
  • c. 1336–1346Vijayanagara founded on the Tungabhadra, beside the shrine of Pampa and Virupaksha at Hampi. Entry →
  • 1342Hoysala Ballala III captured at Kannanur Koppam while campaigning against the Madurai sultanate, and put to death; his kingdom’s remnants pass to Vijayanagara over the next decade. Entry →
  • 3 August 1347Zafar Khan is proclaimed Alauddin Hasan Bahman Shah; the sources differ on the setting, several placing the proclamation at Gulbarga, which became the first Bahmani capital. Entry →
  • 14th–15th c.The war-horse import trade through the west-coast ports; the temple-and-nayankara order in the south; the contested Raichur doab between the two powers. Entry →
  • 1362–1420The first Bahmani–Vijayanagara wars. Entry →
  • c. 1352–1371Kumara Kampana’s southern campaigns for Vijayanagara: the Sambuvarayas reduced, then Madurai conquered; the empire reaches Rameswaram. Entry →
  • c. 1396–1407The ‘Durga Devi famine’ of later Deccan tradition, remembered as a prolonged subsistence crisis; its duration and extent are uncertain.
  • 1398Timur sacks Delhi; the sultanate that had once ruled the Deccan ceases to matter to it.
  • r. 1397–1422Firuz Shah Bahmani; Gesudaraz settles at Gulbarga (1400–1422). Entry →
  • r. 1424–1446Deva Raya II, Vijayanagara’s fifteenth-century peak. Entry →
  • 1425Ahmad Shah I destroys Warangal’s successor state; Telangana passes to the Bahmanis.
  • c. 1425–1432The Bahmani capital moves from Gulbarga to Bidar. Entry →
  • 1442–1444Abdur Razzaq’s embassy to Vijayanagara. Entry →
  • 1446–1465Mallikarjuna and the Sangama decline; Gajapati and Bahmani pressure from the east.
  • 1453–1481Mahmud Gawan’s ascendancy at Bidar; his madrasa built 1472; Goa taken for the Bahmanis 1472; executed 1481. Entry →
  • c. 1469–1472Afanasy Nikitin in the Deccan. Entry →
  • 1482–1528The Bahmani collapse: the Barids keep the last sultans as prisoners at Bidar; the final claimant flees in 1528. Entry →
  • 1485The Saluva usurpation at Vijayanagara. Entry →
  • 20 May 1498Vasco da Gama reaches Calicut. Entry →
  • 1505The second usurpation: Vira Narasimha founds the Tuluva dynasty.

Two Deccans 1500–1565

  • c. 1490–1518Ahmadnagar (founded 1494), Bijapur, Berar, Golconda and Bidar emerge from the Bahmani break-up. Entry →
  • 1490–1528Fathullah Imad-ul-Mulk rules Berar from Elichpur; the Barids govern Bidar behind the last Bahmanis until Kalimullah flees in 1528. Entry →
  • 1500–1509Cabral and da Gama’s second voyage; Almeida viceroy; the naval battle of Diu (1509) establishes Portuguese naval predominance in the western Indian Ocean.
  • 25 November 1510Albuquerque takes Goa; the horse trade passes through Portuguese hands. Entry →
  • 1522 / 1537Shah Tahir reaches Ahmadnagar; Burhan Nizam Shah adopts Twelver Shi’ism as the religion of the state. Entry →
  • c. 1500Vijayanagara at its height, among the largest cities in the world. Entry →
  • r. 1509–1529Krishnadevaraya: the Gajapati war (1512–1519) fixes the Krishna as the eastern frontier; the Amuktamalyada composed. Entry →
  • 19 May 1520The Battle of Raichur. Entry →
  • c. 1520–1522Domingos Paes describes the capital (Nunes’s chronicle c. 1535–37). Entry →
  • 23 December 1534Bahadur Shah of Gujarat cedes Bassein: the Portuguese Província do Norte, Daman to Chaul, lasts until 1739. Entry →
  • 1518–1590Golconda under the early Qutb Shahis; the diamond trade. Entry →
  • 27 July 1550Ibrahim Qutb Shah crowned at Golconda after seven years at Vijayanagara; the Hussain Sagar built 1562. Entry →
  • 1529–1542Achyutadevaraya’s reign, ending in the succession crisis of 1542.
  • 1542–1565Sadashiva crowned as puppet; Rama Raya rules, playing the sultanates against each other. Entry →
  • 1558Vijayanagara and Bijapur sack Ahmadnagar’s country – the provocation remembered before 1565.
  • 1560The Inquisition established at Goa; the First Provincial Council of 1567 follows. Entry →
  • January 1565Talikota: the army broken in a day, the capital sacked in the months that followed. Entry →
  • 1565–mid-17th c.Aravidu Vijayanagara: Tirumala establishes the court at Penukonda and is crowned in 1570; the capital moves to Chandragiri c. 1592 and later Vellore; the line fades in the 1640s.

The Mughal South 1565–1687

  • 1574 / 1619Ahmadnagar absorbs Berar; Bijapur later absorbs Bidar. Entry →
  • r. 1580–1627Ibrahim Adil Shah II at Bijapur; the Kitab-i-Nauras; Dakhni’s literary flowering. Entry →
  • 1591Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah founds Hyderabad. Entry →
  • 1595–1600Chand Bibi defends Ahmadnagar; Akbar takes the city in 1600. Entry →
  • c. 1548–1626Malik Ambar’s defence of the Nizam Shahi state; Khirki (later Aurangabad) founded 1610; Bhatvadi 1624. Entry →
  • c. 1608–1650Tukaram at Dehu; the Varkari tradition’s seventeenth-century flowering (Ramdas 1608–1681). Entry →
  • 1611–1668The Company’s first Deccan footholds: Masulipatnam, Madras, Bombay. Entry →
  • 1594–1664Shahji Bhonsle between Ahmadnagar, Bijapur and the Mughals. Entry →
  • 1630–1632The great Deccan famine; Daulatabad falls 1633; the Nizam Shahi state extinguished 1636. Entry →
  • 1646Shivaji takes Torna. Entry →
  • 1656–1657Bijapur’s succession crisis; Aurangzeb’s first invasion, broken off for the war of succession at Delhi; the Gol Gumbaz completed. Entry →
  • 10 November 1659Afzal Khan killed below Pratapgad. Entry →
  • 1660Siddi Jauhar besieges Panhala; the night escape to Vishalgad and Baji Prabhu’s stand at the pass.
  • 1663–1664The night raid on Shaista Khan; the sack of Surat; Sindhudurg begun. Entry →
  • 1665–1666The Treaty of Purandar; Agra, confinement and escape. Entry →
  • 1670–1672Sinhagad retaken (Tanaji Malusare); the second sack of Surat; Salher, the first great open-field victory.
  • 6 June 1674The coronation at Raigad; the Ashtapradhan. Entry →
  • c. 1674–1686Madanna and Akkanna run Golconda; Shivaji’s Hyderabad treaty and Karnataka campaign 1677. Entry →
  • 3 April 1680Shivaji dies at Raigad; the succession contested.
  • 1681Prince Akbar’s rebellion brings Aurangzeb south for good.
  • 1686–1687Bijapur surrenders; Golconda falls by treachery. Entry →
  • 1657–1689Sambhaji: nine years’ war on every front; captured at Sangameshwar and executed 11 March 1689 – the hinge into the next period. Entry →

The Maratha Century 1687–1761

  • 1690–1698The siege of Jinji; Rajaram’s government in exile. Entry →
  • c. 1695–1710Papadu’s bandit kingdom in Mughal Telangana; Warangal sacked 1708. Entry →
  • 1699–1705Aurangzeb’s last campaign: the Maratha forts taken one by one, and lost again.
  • 1700–1707Rajaram dies; Tarabai carries on the war. Entry →
  • 3 March 1707Aurangzeb dies at Ahmadnagar; Shahu released; the battle of Khed. Entry →
  • 1708–1749Chhatrapati and Peshwa: Satara, and the rise of the Bhat family; the Treaty of Warna divides Satara and Kolhapur 1731. Entry →
  • 1713–1729Kanhoji Angre commands the coast; the Ajnapatra written 1715. Entry →
  • 1719Shahu’s sanads: chauth and sardeshmukhi of the six subahs. Entry →
  • 11 October 1724Shakarkheda: Nizam-ul-Mulk establishes the Asaf Jahi state. Entry →
  • 28 February 1728Palkhed: Bajirao outmanoeuvres the Nizam. Entry →
  • 1700–1740Bajirao I: Bhopal 1737, the Delhi raid, the northern expansion (Nadir Shah sacks Delhi 1739). Entry →
  • February–May 1739Bassein: the Portuguese northern province falls to Chimaji Appa. Entry →
  • 1728–1761Holkar, Scindia, Gaekwad, Bhonsle: the confederacy takes shape. Entry →
  • 1746–1763The Carnatic wars; Arcot 1751. Entry →
  • 1751–1758Bussy and the French at Hyderabad; the Circars to the French 1753, to the Company 1765–66. Entry →
  • February 1756Vijaydurg (Gheria): Company and Peshwa forces destroy Tulaji Angre’s fleet; the Maratha navy broken as a force.
  • 3 February 1760Udgir: the Nizam’s great cession. Entry →
  • 14 January 1761Panipat; Haidar Ali completes his rise at Mysore in the same years. Entry →
  • 1762Nizam Ali deposes Salabat Jang. Entry →

Mysore and the Company Wars 1761–1799

  • 1761–1772Madhavrao I and the recovery after Panipat; Rakshasbhuvan (10 August 1763) restores the balance against the Nizam. Entry →
  • 1762–1763Nizam Ali deposes Salabat Jang (8 July 1762) and moves the capital from Aurangabad to Hyderabad (1763). Entry →
  • c. 1730–1803The bankers of the Deccan: Gujarati, gosain and Marwari houses at Hyderabad, the Peshwa’s sahukars at Pune, the Company’s creditors at Bombay. Entry →
  • 1767–1769The first Anglo-Mysore war ends at Madras on Haidar’s terms. Entry →
  • 30 August 1773Peshwa Narayanrao murdered in the Shaniwar Wada; Raghunathrao’s usurpation and the Barbhai council.
  • 1742–1800Nana Phadnavis manages the Pune state through the interregnum. Entry →
  • 1779–1782Wadgaon and Salbai: the first Anglo-Maratha war. Entry →
  • 10 September 1780Pollilur: Baillie’s column destroyed (Porto Novo, 1781, turns the war). Entry →
  • 1782–1784Haidar dies; Tipu succeeds; the Treaty of Mangalore. Entry →
  • 1784–1799Tipu’s state: reforms, rockets, embassies. Entry →
  • 1791–1792The Doji Bara, the skull famine, across the Deccan and the Circars after four failed monsoons. Entry →
  • 18 March 1792The Treaty of Seringapatam: half of Mysore divided among the allies. Entry →
  • 12 February 1794Mahadji Scindia dies at Pune, at the height of his power in the north. Entry →
  • 11 March 1795Kharda: the confederacy’s last joint victory over the Nizam. Entry →
  • 1795–1796Madhavrao II’s death; Bajirao II becomes Peshwa; the factional decade begins.
  • 1 September 1798The subsidiary alliance at Hyderabad. Entry →
  • 4 May 1799Srirangapatna stormed; Tipu killed near the Water Gate. Entry →

Paramountcy 1799–1831

  • 30 June 1799Mysore restored under the Wodeyars; Mackenzie’s surveys and collection begin. Entry →
  • 1799–1800Dhondia Wagh’s rising across the Mysore–Maratha frontier, hunted down by Wellesley.
  • 1800The Nizam cedes the Rayalaseema districts – the ‘Ceded Districts’ – to pay for the subsidiary force.
  • 10 April 1802Lambton measures his first baseline; the Great Trigonometrical Survey begins. Entry →
  • 25 October 1802Yashwantrao Holkar defeats the Peshwa and Scindia outside Pune; Bajirao II flees to the Company. Entry →
  • 31 December 1802The Treaty of Bassein: restoration at the price of a subsidiary force. Entry →
  • 23 September 1803Assaye; Argaon and Gawilghur follow; the second Anglo-Maratha war ends in 1805 after Holkar’s separate campaign. Entry →
  • 1803–1820sThe Hyderabad Residency: indirect rule made architecture. Entry →
  • 1816–1824Palmer and Company at Hyderabad: Hastings’s licence, Metcalfe’s audit, the crore of 1823, bankruptcy. Entry →
  • 1817–1818The Pindari campaign and the third Anglo-Maratha war: Kirkee (5 November 1817), Koregaon (1 January 1818), Ashti (19/20 February 1818). Entry →
  • 3 June 1818The Peshwa surrenders; Satara restored as a small raj; the Deccan Commission begins its work. Entry →
  • 25 October 1819Elphinstone’s Report on the conquered territories. Entry →
  • 1824The Kittur rising (Rani Chennamma) in the southern Maratha country.
  • 1826–1831Umaji Naik’s Ramoshi rising around Pune; executed 1832.
  • 1827The Elphinstone Code: the Bombay Regulations consolidate the law of the conquered territories. Entry →
  • 1830–1831The Nagar revolt; direct administration of Mysore begins. Entry →

The Settlement 1831–1875

  • 1831The inheritance of grants; jagirs and saranjams pass under Company rules. Entry →
  • 1835–1847The Bombay revenue survey: Pringle’s failure, the Goldsmid–Wingate system. Entry →
  • 1844–1845The Gadkari rising at Kolhapur against the new revenue terms.
  • 1848Satara annexed by lapse; Phule’s girls’ school opens at Pune. Entry →
  • 1852–1863The Inam Commission examines the Deccan’s grants. Entry →
  • 21 May 1853The Berar assignment; Salar Jang becomes diwan of Hyderabad in the same month. Entry →
  • 1853–1883Salar Jang remakes the Nizam’s government: districts, courts, departments, schools. Entry →
  • 1853–1863The railway: Bombay–Thane 1853; the Bhor Ghat incline opens the Deccan 1863. Entry →
  • 1857–18581857 in the Deccan: Satara conspiracy, Kolhapur mutinies, Nargund; the attack on the Hyderabad Residency repulsed. Entry →
  • 1 November 1858The Crown takes over; the Queen’s Proclamation. Entry →
  • 1861–1865The cotton boom and bust; the Bombay crash of 1865. Entry →
  • 1867 / 1881The Mysore question; rendition follows in 1881. Entry →
  • 1873Phule’s Gulamgiri; the Satyashodhak Samaj founded 24 September. Entry →
  • 1874–1875The Watan Act in force February 1875. Entry →
