Period 05 · 1761–1799

Mysore and the Company Wars

Haidar Ali and Tipu Sultan build the Deccan’s last serious counter-project while the Company learns to be a territorial power.

Panipat shattered Maratha manpower, credit and central leadership; the Peshwa’s government recovered much under Madhavrao, but the confederacy grew more centrifugal, and four powers now contended in the south: the Marathas, the Nizam, the Company at Madras and Bombay, and a new state in Mysore under Haidar Ali, a soldier who had seized effective control of the kingdom while keeping the Wodeyar raja as a figurehead. Haidar and his son Tipu fought the Company four times in thirty years, twice to a draw. Tipu’s Mysore was a centralising, experimenting state – rockets, silk, a new calendar, embassies to Constantinople and Paris – and the Company, which had learnt at Wadgaon what a Maratha army could do, treated it as the chief danger in India. The period ends with the storming of Srirangapatna and Tipu killed in the fighting near the Water Gate.

  • 1761–1772 Madhavrao I

    The fourth Peshwa, who took office at sixteen after Panipat and in eleven years rebuilt Maratha power in the Deccan and the north.

    Person · Marathas · Hyderabad · Mysore

  • c. 1730–1803 The bankers of the Deccan

    Sahukars and shroffs lent to the Peshwa, the Nizam and the Company alike, moved their money by hundi and took assignments of land revenue as security; the ‘great firm’ argument and its critics.

    Institution · Marathas · Hyderabad · East India Company · Other

  • r. 1762–1803 Nizam Ali Khan and the move to Hyderabad

    Asaf Jah II deposed his brother in 1762, moved the capital from Aurangabad to Hyderabad in 1763, fought the Marathas, sold the Circars for troops and ended as the first prince inside Wellesley’s subsidiary system.

    Person · Hyderabad · Marathas · East India Company

  • 1767–1769 The first Anglo-Mysore war

    Haidar Ali fought the Company and its allies to a standstill and dictated peace at the gates of Madras in 1769.

    Treaty · Mysore · East India Company · Hyderabad · Marathas

  • 1610–1799 Srirangapatna

    The island fortress in the Kaveri that was the seat of the Wodeyars, Haidar Ali and Tipu Sultan, and the prize of 1792 and 1799.

    Place · Mysore · Vijayanagara · East India Company

  • 1742–1800 Nana Phadnavis

    The minister who ran the Pune government for a quarter of a century in the name of a child Peshwa and kept the Company at arm’s length.

    Person · Marathas · East India Company · Hyderabad · Mysore

  • 1779–1782 Wadgaon and Salbai

    A Company army surrendered to the Marathas in the Western Ghats in 1779; the Treaty of Salbai ended the first Anglo-Maratha war three years later.

    Treaty · Marathas · East India Company

  • 1780–1799 The Mysore rocket

    Iron-cased rockets fired by Haidar and Tipu’s armies, decisive at Pollilur in 1780 and copied at Woolwich after 1799.

    Object · Mysore · East India Company

  • 1782–1784 Tipu Sultan and the Treaty of Mangalore

    Haidar Ali died in the field in December 1782; his son ended the second Anglo-Mysore war on equal terms at Mangalore in March 1784.

    Treaty · Mysore · East India Company

  • 1784–1799 Tipu’s state

    The revenue regulations, state trading, new coinage, silk industry and Mauludi calendar by which Tipu tried to make Mysore a sovereign state of a new kind.

    Institution · Mysore

  • 1786–1798 Tipu’s embassies

    Missions to Constantinople and Versailles, and the so-called Jacobin club of Srirangapatna, in Tipu’s search for allies beyond India.

    Document · Mysore · Other · East India Company

  • 1791–1792 The Doji Bara famine

    Three failed monsoons from 1789 and a poor fourth produced the ‘skull famine’ across the Deccan, Hyderabad and the Circars in the middle of the third Mysore war; the Company’s first famine measures in the south were made in the same months as the Treaty of Seringapatam.

    Event · Marathas · Hyderabad · Mysore · East India Company

  • c. 1793 Tipu’s Tiger

    A mechanical organ in the form of a tiger killing a European, taken from Tipu’s palace in 1799 and displayed in London since the early nineteenth century.

    Object · Mysore · East India Company

  • 18 March 1792 The Treaty of Seringapatam

    Cornwallis’s coalition took half of Mysore and two of Tipu’s sons as hostages at the end of the third Anglo-Mysore war.

    Treaty · Mysore · East India Company · Hyderabad · Marathas

  • 1794–1795 Mahadji Scindia and Kharda

    The death of the confederacy’s greatest soldier in 1794 and, a year later, the last united Maratha campaign, which humbled the Nizam at Kharda.

    Battle · Marathas · Hyderabad · Mughal empire · East India Company

  • 1 September 1798 The subsidiary alliance

    Wellesley’s treaty with the Nizam, the model for the system by which Indian states paid for the Company’s troops and gave up their foreign relations.

    Institution · Hyderabad · East India Company

  • 4 May 1799 Seringapatam, 1799

    The storming of Tipu’s capital by Harris’s army and the Nizam’s contingent, in which Tipu Sultan was killed and the Mysore sultanate ended.

    Battle · Mysore · East India Company · Hyderabad

  • 10 September 1780 Pollilur, 1780

    Haidar Ali and Tipu annihilated Baillie's column near Kanchipuram – the Company's worst defeat in India to that date, remembered in captivity narratives and painted on Tipu's palace wall.

    Battle · Mysore · East India Company
