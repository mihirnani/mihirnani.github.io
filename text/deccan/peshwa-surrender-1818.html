The Peshwa surrenders

3 June 1818 · Event · Bithur, Uttar Pradesh

Bajirao II gave himself up to John Malcolm, accepted a pension and left the Deccan for a village on the Ganges, and the office of Peshwa was abolished.

On 3 June 1818 the Peshwa Bajirao II surrendered to Sir John Malcolm at the edge of the Narmada country, after six months of flight across the Deccan with the Company’s columns behind him. Malcolm, acting on his own judgement, granted terms more generous than Hastings had wanted: a pension of eight lakhs of rupees a year, retention of his personal property and a residence of his choosing outside Maharashtra. Bajirao went to Bithur, near Kanpur on the Ganges, and lived there until his death in 1851. His adopted son Nana Sahib, refused the pension, became a leader of the rising of 1857.

The office of Peshwa, created by the Chhatrapatis of Satara in the late seventeenth century and held by the Bhat family since 1713, was extinguished. Its territories, stretching from the Konkan across the Desh to the Karnatak, passed directly to the Company and were organised as the Deccan conquered provinces under Elphinstone. The Peshwa’s feudatories and hereditary officers were confirmed, pensioned or dismissed on a case by case basis; his forts were garrisoned; the Shaniwar Wada in Pune became Company property.

The houses of the confederacy survived the war as subsidiary states: Scindia at Gwalior, Holkar at Indore, the Gaekwad at Baroda and a diminished Bhonsle at Nagpur, each now bound by treaty and watched by a Resident. What they no longer had was a head. With the Peshwa gone, the Maratha polity ceased to exist as a system, and the Company took the position at its centre that the Peshwa had occupied since the sanads of 1719.

In the story

The surrender marks the removal of the last layer that had organised Maratha sovereignty. Since 1719 the Peshwa had stood between the Chhatrapati and the confederacy, holding Mughal sanads for chauth and sardeshmukhi, granting saranjams, arbitrating between houses. The Company inherited that position without the title. Sovereignty in western India had been a set of nested claims, Mughal, Chhatrapati, Peshwa, sardar. After June 1818 they were pensions, treaties and files.
