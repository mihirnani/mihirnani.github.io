1327–1900

  • c. 1325–1475 The Reddis and Velamas

    After the Kakatiya fall in 1323, Telugu warrior lineages at Kondavidu, Rajahmundry and Rachakonda ruled coastal Andhra between the two great powers.

    Institution · Other · Bahmani sultanate · Vijayanagara

  • 1327 · Prologue Tughluq moves the capital to Daulatabad

    In 1327 Muhammad bin Tughluq ordered the Delhi court to Devagiri, renamed Daulatabad, to rule the Deccan from inside it; within a decade the experiment had failed.

    Event · Delhi sultanate

  • c. 1335–1378 Madurai and the southern conquest

    Delhi's southernmost province became an independent sultanate about 1335; a generation later Kumara Kampana conquered it for Vijayanagara, and a Tungabhadra kingdom became an empire of the whole peninsula.

    Event · Vijayanagara · Delhi sultanate · Other

  • c. 1336–1346 The founding of Vijayanagara

    Between about 1336 and 1346 the brothers Harihara and Bukka built a kingdom on the Tungabhadra that became Vijayanagara; how they did so is largely tradition.

    Event · Vijayanagara

  • before 1500 Virupaksha and the sacred centre

    The Virupaksha temple and the older Pampa cult on the Tungabhadra, the sacred ground the Sangamas built their capital beside.

    Place · Vijayanagara

  • 14th–15th century Horses before the Portuguese

    Arab and Persian horses landed at Honavar, Bhatkal and the Konkan ports were the one import both Deccan states could not do without.

    Institution · Vijayanagara · Bahmani sultanate · Other

  • 3 August 1347 The Bahmani revolt

    In 1347 the rebel amirs of the Deccan made Zafar Khan their sultan as Alauddin Hasan Bahman Shah, ending Delhi’s rule south of the Vindhyas.

    Event · Bahmani sultanate · Delhi sultanate

  • 1347–c. 1430 Gulbarga

    Gulbarga, renamed Ahsanabad, was the first Bahmani capital from 1347 until the court moved to Bidar around 1430; its fort and Jami Masjid survive.

    Place · Bahmani sultanate

  • c. 1350–1565 The Raichur doab

    The wedge of land between the Krishna and the Tungabhadra, with its forts at Raichur and Mudgal, was the standing frontier of the Deccan for two centuries.

    Place · Bahmani sultanate · Vijayanagara · Deccan sultanates

  • 14th–15th century Temples and the nayankara

    How Vijayanagara governed: temple endowment as political economy, military tenure for its chiefs, and a long scholarly argument about what kind of state it was.

    Institution · Vijayanagara

  • 1362–1420 The Bahmani–Vijayanagara wars

    Between 1362 and 1420 the Bahmanis and Vijayanagara fought a series of wars over the doab and the coast; the chroniclers’ accounts of them need care.

    Event · Bahmani sultanate · Vijayanagara

  • r. 1397–1422 Firuz Shah Bahmani

    Taj-ud-din Firuz Shah, sultan from 1397 to 1422, opened the Bahmani court to Persian talent, built Firuzabad on the Bhima and, by Firishta’s account, married a Vijayanagara princess.

    Person · Bahmani sultanate · Vijayanagara

  • 1400–1422 Gesudaraz at Gulbarga

    The Chishti saint Sayyid Muhammad Gesudaraz, who settled at Gulbarga in 1400 and died there in 1422, made the Bahmani capital a centre of shrine devotion.

    Person · Bahmani sultanate

  • 15th century Kannada and Telugu at court

    Kumara Vyasa's Kannada Bharata and Srinatha's Telugu prabandhas: the two languages of the southern Deccan made literary under royal and Reddi patronage.

    Document · Vijayanagara · Other

  • r. 1424–1446 Deva Raya II

    Deva Raya II, who ruled Vijayanagara from 1424 to 1446, took the state to its first height and recruited Muslim archers and horsemen into its army.

    Person · Vijayanagara · Bahmani sultanate

  • c. 1430–1500 Afaqis and Dakhnis

    The split between immigrant ‘Westerners’ and Deccan-born nobles at the Bahmani court, which turned to massacre in 1446 and 1481 and outlived the dynasty.

    Institution · Bahmani sultanate

  • c. 1430–1619 Bidar

    The second Bahmani capital on its basalt plateau: fort, palaces, the tombs at Ashtur, and the Baridi ministers who inherited it.

    Place · Bahmani sultanate · Deccan sultanates

  • c. 1425–1432 The move to Bidar

    Around 1430 Ahmad Shah I moved the Bahmani capital from Gulbarga to Bidar, renamed Muhammadabad, and rebuilt its fort on a new scale.

    Event · Bahmani sultanate

  • 1442–1444 Abdur Razzaq at Vijayanagara

    The Timurid envoy Abdur Razzaq Samarqandi visited Calicut and Vijayanagara in 1442–44 and described both in his Matla-us-Sadain.

    Document · Vijayanagara · Other

  • 1453–1481 Mahmud Gawan

    The Iranian merchant who became the Bahmani sultanate's chief minister, reorganised its provinces, took Goa, and was executed on a forged letter in 1481.

    Person · Bahmani sultanate

  • c. 1469–1472 Afanasy Nikitin in the Deccan

    A merchant from Tver who spent about three years in the Bahmani Deccan and left the first Russian account of India, the Journey Beyond Three Seas.

    Document · Bahmani sultanate · Other

  • 1472 The Madrasa of Mahmud Gawan

    A three-storeyed college built in Bidar by the Bahmani vizier on the model of Timurid Central Asia, tiled in blue and green and now half ruined.

    Place · Bahmani sultanate

  • 1482–1528 The Bahmani collapse

    Between the death of Muhammad III in 1482 and the flight of the last nominal sultan in 1528, the Bahmani governors became kings and the minister Qasim Barid kept the crown as a prisoner.

    Event · Bahmani sultanate · Deccan sultanates

  • 1485 The Saluva usurpation

    Saluva Narasimha, a general from Chandragiri, removed the last Sangama king in 1485; within twenty years his own line had been set aside by the Tuluvas.

    Event · Vijayanagara

  • c. 1490–1518 The Bahmani break-up

    Between about 1490 and 1518 the Bahmani sultanate at Bidar dissolved into five successor states: Bijapur, Ahmadnagar, Berar, Golconda and Bidar itself.

    Institution · Deccan sultanates

  • 1490–1619 Berar and Bidar

    The two lesser successor states of the Bahmani break-up: Imad Shahi Berar, ruled from Elichpur and swallowed by Ahmadnagar in 1574, and Barid Shahi Bidar, a capital without a country, taken by Bijapur in 1619.

    Institution · Deccan sultanates

  • 1490–1565 Ahmadnagar

    Founded by Malik Ahmad in 1494 as the capital of the Nizam Shahi sultanate, Ahmadnagar was the most Marathi of the successor states and the school of the families who later built the Maratha state.

    Place · Deccan sultanates · Marathas

  • 20 May 1498 The Portuguese at Calicut

    Vasco da Gama anchored off Calicut in May 1498 and met the Zamorin; twelve years later the Portuguese held Goa and a place in the Deccan.

    Event · Portuguese · Other

  • 16th century The horse trade

    Warhorses shipped from Arabia and the Gulf through Goa, Bhatkal and the Konkan ports were the Deccan’s essential military import, and control of them was a matter of state.

    Institution · Vijayanagara · Deccan sultanates · Portuguese · Other

  • c. 1500 Vijayanagara, the city

    The capital on the Tungabhadra, known today as Hampi, was around 1500 among the largest cities in the world, built of granite, water and temples across a landscape of boulders.

    Place · Vijayanagara

  • r. 1509–1529 Krishnadevaraya

    Krishnadevaraya of the Tuluva dynasty ruled Vijayanagara from 1509 to 1529, the reign in which the empire reached its greatest extent and its capital its greatest size.

    Person · Vijayanagara

  • 25 November 1510 Albuquerque takes Goa

    Afonso de Albuquerque seized Goa from Bijapur in 1510, giving the Portuguese a permanent base and a grip on the sea-borne horse trade to the Deccan.

    Event · Portuguese · Deccan sultanates

  • 1510–1553 Burhan Nizam Shah and Shah Tahir

    A child-sultan who reigned for four decades, an Ismaili refugee from Safavid Iran who made his court Shia in 1537, and the shifting alliances with Bijapur and Vijayanagara that led to the marriages of 1564.

    Person · Deccan sultanates · Vijayanagara

  • c. 1510s–1520s Amuktamalyada

    Krishnadevaraya’s Telugu court poem on the saint Andal contains a long passage of advice to kings, the nearest thing Vijayanagara left to a statement of how it was meant to be governed.

    Document · Vijayanagara

  • 1518–1590 Golconda and its diamonds

    The granite fortress of Golconda was the Qutb Shahi capital from 1518 until the founding of Hyderabad in 1591, and the market through which the Krishna valley’s diamonds reached the world.

    Place · Deccan sultanates

  • 19 May 1520 The Battle of Raichur

    Krishnadevaraya defeated Ismail Adil Shah of Bijapur outside Raichur in May 1520 and took the fort, in a campaign that marks the first significant appearance of cannon and the earliest known use of matchlocks in South Asian warfare.

    Battle · Vijayanagara · Deccan sultanates · Portuguese

  • c. 1520–1522 Domingos Paes describes Vijayanagara

    A Portuguese merchant’s eyewitness account of Krishnadevaraya’s capital, written about 1520–22 and paired with Fernão Nunes’s chronicle of the 1530s, is the fullest outside description of Vijayanagara.

    Document · Vijayanagara · Portuguese

  • c. 1529–1565 and after The nayakas

    The military governors Vijayanagara placed over Madurai, Thanjavur, Gingee and Keladi became, after 1565, kings in their own right and the successor states of the southern Deccan.

    Institution · Vijayanagara · Other

  • 1534–1739 Bassein and the Província do Norte

    Bahadur Shah of Gujarat ceded Bassein to the Portuguese on 23 December 1534; for two centuries the villages between Daman and Chaul were one of the Estado da Índia's largest agrarian possessions in India, with revenue and a landed settler class of its own.

    Place · Portuguese · Deccan sultanates · Other

  • 1490–1558 Bijapur under the Adil Shahis

    From Yusuf Adil Khan’s independence in 1490 to the death of Ibrahim I in 1558, Bijapur built the strongest of the successor sultanates while its court swung between Persian and Deccani factions.

    Place · Deccan sultanates

  • 1542–1565 Rama Raya

    Krishnadevaraya’s son-in-law ruled Vijayanagara as regent for the puppet king Sadasiva from 1542, played the sultanates against one another for twenty years and died at Talikota.

    Person · Vijayanagara · Deccan sultanates

  • r. 1550–1580 Ibrahim Qutb Shah

    Seven years a guest at Vijayanagara, thirty a sultan at Golconda: the Qutb Shahi ruler who was praised in Telugu as Malkibharama, built the Hussain Sagar, and marched against his former host in 1565.

    Person · Deccan sultanates · Vijayanagara

  • 1560–1812 The Goa Inquisition

    The tribunal of the Holy Office set up at Goa in 1560 tried converts and New Christians for two and a half centuries; its numbers are known only from the lists that survived the destruction of its archive.

    Institution · Portuguese

  • January 1565 Talikota, 1565

    A coalition of the sultanates defeated Vijayanagara’s army near the Krishna on 23 January 1565, killed Rama Raya and sacked the capital; the empire survived in the south for another century.

    Battle · Vijayanagara · Deccan sultanates

  • 1591 Hyderabad founded

    Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah lays out a new open city on the Musi, with the Charminar at its crossing, a few miles from the fortress of Golconda.

    Place · Deccan sultanates · Hyderabad

  • 1595–1600 Chand Bibi and the siege of Ahmadnagar

    The Adil Shahi dowager who held Ahmadnagar against Prince Murad in 1595–96, ceded Berar, and was killed by her own soldiers before the city fell.

    Person · Deccan sultanates · Mughal empire

  • August 1600 Akbar takes Ahmadnagar

    Event · Mughal empire · Deccan sultanates

  • c. 1400–1700 Dakhni

    The vernacular of the Deccan sultanates, carried by Sufi shrines like Gesudaraz’s at Gulbarga, and the shared court culture it sustained from Bijapur to Golconda.

    Institution · Deccan sultanates · Other

  • r. 1580–1627 Ibrahim Adil Shah II and the Kitab-i-Nauras

    The sixth Adil Shahi sultan of Bijapur, musician, patron of painting and author of a Dakhni songbook that invokes Saraswati alongside the Prophet.

    Person · Deccan sultanates

  • c. 1608–1650 Tukaram and the Varkari tradition

    Person · Deccan sultanates · Marathas · Other

  • c. 1548–1626 Malik Ambar

    An Ethiopian-born slave who became regent of Ahmadnagar, held off the Mughals for a quarter-century and trained the Maratha cavalry that later served Shivaji.

    Person · Deccan sultanates · Mughal empire · Marathas

  • 1611–1668 Masulipatnam, Madras, Bombay

    The East India Company’s first Deccan footholds: a factory at Masulipatnam under Golconda, a fort at Madras from 1639, and the Portuguese island of Bombay from 1668.

    Place · East India Company · Deccan sultanates · Mughal empire · Portuguese

  • 1630–1632 The Deccan famine of 1630–32

    Event · Mughal empire · Deccan sultanates

    Treaty · Mughal empire · Deccan sultanates

  • 1594–1664 Shahji Bhonsle

    Person · Deccan sultanates · Mughal empire · Marathas

  • 1646 Shivaji takes Torna

    A sixteen-year-old Shivaji seizes a Bijapuri hill fort above Pune, the first of the strongholds from which a new state would be built.

    Event · Marathas · Deccan sultanates

  • 1656 Gol Gumbaz

    The tomb of Muhammad Adil Shah at Bijapur, a single cube under one of the largest masonry domes ever built, left unfinished at his death in 1656.

    Place · Deccan sultanates

    Battle · Marathas · Deccan sultanates

    Shivaji’s night raid on the Mughal viceroy at Pune and his six-day plunder of Surat, the empire’s richest port, announce him to the Mughals and the Company.

    Event · Marathas · Mughal empire · East India Company

  • 1664–1667 The Maratha navy

    Shivaji’s sea fort at Malvan, begun in November 1664 with the Surat plunder, and the fleet he built to face the Siddis of Janjira and the Europeans.

    Place · Marathas · Portuguese · Other

  • 11 June 1665 The Treaty of Purandar and Agra

    Shivaji surrenders twenty-three forts to Jai Singh, goes to Aurangzeb’s court at Agra, is confined, and escapes – by the best-known account, hidden in a basket of sweets.

    Treaty · Marathas · Mughal empire

  • c. 1674 The Ashtapradhan

    Shivaji’s council of eight ministers, and the Sanskrit lexicon that renamed the Persian vocabulary of Deccan government.

    Institution · Marathas

    Shivaji is consecrated as Chhatrapati by the Benares scholar Gaga Bhatt in a Vedic rite at his capital of Raigad, with an English envoy among the witnesses.

    Event · Marathas

  • c. 1674–1686 Madanna and Akkanna at Golconda

  • 1686–1687 Aurangzeb takes Bijapur and Golconda

    Event · Mughal empire · Deccan sultanates

  • 1657–1689 Sambhaji

    Shivaji’s elder son and successor, who fought the Mughals, the Siddis and the Portuguese for nine years, was captured at Sangameshwar and executed in March 1689.

    Person · Marathas · Mughal empire · Portuguese

  • 1690–1698 The siege of Jinji

    An eight-year Mughal siege of the Maratha court in exile at Jinji, in the far south, that ended with the fort taken and the king gone.

    Event · Marathas · Mughal empire

  • c. 1695–1710 Papadu

    Person · Mughal empire · Other

  • 1675–1761 Tarabai

    Person · Marathas · Mughal empire

  • 3 March 1707 Aurangzeb dies at Ahmadnagar

  • 1708–1749 Chhatrapati and Peshwa

  • c. 1669–1729 Kanhoji Angre

  • 1719 Shahu, Balaji Vishwanath and the sanads of 1719

  • 1700–1740 Bajirao I
