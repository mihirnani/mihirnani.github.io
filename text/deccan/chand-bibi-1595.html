Chand Bibi and the siege of Ahmadnagar

1595–1600 · Person · Ahmadnagar, Maharashtra

The Adil Shahi dowager who held Ahmadnagar against Prince Murad in 1595–96, ceded Berar, and was killed by her own soldiers before the city fell.

Chand Bibi was a daughter of Hussain Nizam Shah I of Ahmadnagar, married in 1564 to Ali Adil Shah I of Bijapur as part of the alliance that preceded Talikota. Widowed in 1580, she acted as regent for the boy Ibrahim Adil Shah II before returning to Ahmadnagar, where a succession crisis in 1595 left her as guardian of her infant great-nephew Bahadur. That autumn Akbar’s son Murad, with Abdur Rahim Khan-i-Khanan, marched on the city.

The siege began in November 1595. Chand Bibi is recorded as having directed the defence in person, repairing a breach in the wall under fire; the Mughal accounts, which are the fullest, make her the central figure. Short of supplies on both sides, the parties settled in February 1596: Ahmadnagar recognised Bahadur Nizam Shah and ceded Berar, the sultanate’s richest province, to the Mughals. Murad withdrew.

The settlement did not hold. Fighting over Berar resumed, Murad died in 1599, and Akbar sent his other son Daniyal and the Khan-i-Khanan back to Ahmadnagar. Factional struggle inside the city undid it. Chand Bibi is said to have opened negotiations; a rumour spread that she was selling the fort, and she was killed by her own troops, in 1599 or 1600 according to different chroniclers. The Mughals stormed Ahmadnagar in August 1600 and took Bahadur to Gwalior as a prisoner.

Her death removed the last figure able to hold the Nizam Shahi factions together, and the fall of the capital began the Mughal presence in the Deccan that is the theme of this period. Chand Bibi herself became a figure of later legend, painted and retold in Hyderabad and in Maratha ballads; the historical record is thinner than the legend but the siege is well attested.

In the story

Chand Bibi moved between Bijapur and Ahmadnagar as daughter, wife, regent and guardian, a career only possible in a Deccan where the sultanates were bound by marriage as well as war. Her defence of Ahmadnagar is the moment when that system of peer states first met an empire that did not treat them as peers. The cession of Berar was the first piece of Deccan territory to pass under Mughal rule, and it set the pattern of piecemeal absorption that ended at Golconda in 1687.
