The coronation at Raigad

6 June 1674 · Event · Raigad, Maharashtra

Shivaji is consecrated as Chhatrapati by the Benares scholar Gaga Bhatt in a Vedic rite at his capital of Raigad, with an English envoy among the witnesses.

On 6 June 1674 Shivaji was crowned at Raigad, the hill fort in the Konkan that he had made his capital. The rite was a full Vedic consecration, abhisheka, conducted by Gaga Bhatt, a Brahmin scholar brought from Benares, and it gave Shivaji the title Chhatrapati, lord of the umbrella. He was weighed against gold and other goods that were distributed as gifts, and a new era, the Rajyabhisheka Shaka, was proclaimed from the date. A second, tantric ceremony was performed on 24 September by Nischal Puri to correct defects in the first, including the death of Jijabai within days of it.

The ceremony had to solve a problem of status. The Bhonsles were a family of Maratha deshmukhs, and the Brahmins of Maharashtra had been reluctant to treat them as Kshatriyas, the warrior order from which kings were supposed to come. Gaga Bhatt supplied a genealogy tracing them to the Sisodia Rajputs of Mewar and performed the thread ceremony that the rite required. The expense was considerable, and the English at Bombay, who sent Henry Oxinden to attend and negotiate a trading agreement, recorded the gifts, the processions and the cost in their letters, which remain the fullest outside account.

Until 1674 Shivaji had been, in legal terms, a rebel jagirdar of Bijapur who had briefly held a Mughal mansab. After it, he was a king by the oldest Indian form of title, with his own seal, currency and era, and the basis on which he could grant land and demand allegiance was no longer in doubt. His officers were reorganised as the Ashtapradhan council at the same time.

He died at Raigad on 3 April 1680, less than six years later. The title he had taken passed to Sambhaji, and then through Rajaram, Tarabai and Shahu to the Satara line that the Company pensioned in 1818.

In the story

A coronation is a claim addressed to others, and Shivaji’s was addressed at once to the Mughals, to Bijapur, to the Brahmins and to the Company’s agent in the crowd. By taking a Sanskrit title and a Rajput pedigree he placed the new state in a register the sultanates and the empire did not control. The collection marks 1674 as the moment a further layer of sovereignty was formally added to the Deccan, one the Company would still be dealing with at Satara in the 1830s.
