Pune under the Company

1818–1830s · Place · Pune, Maharashtra

The Peshwa’s capital became a cantonment town and a district headquarters of the Bombay Presidency, its palace a burnt shell.

Pune passed to the Company in November 1817 and was formally annexed in 1818. Within months the army laid out a cantonment on open ground east of the city, on the far side of the Mutha river from the old town, with a second at Khadki to the north where the battle of 1817 had been fought. The cantonments brought a garrison, barracks, a bazaar, a church and a European population that lived apart from the Peshwa’s city of wadas and temples. The Collector’s office and courts were established, and the districts of the Desh were administered from Pune under the Deccan Commission and then, from 1826, as part of the Bombay Presidency. In the decades that followed the governors of Bombay took to spending the monsoon months in Pune.

The city itself was left largely alone. The Shaniwar Wada, seat of the Peshwas since 1732, was occupied by the Company; on 27 February 1828 a fire broke out inside it and burned for seven days, leaving the granite ramparts and teak gateways standing over a ruin. The cause was never explained. In 1821 Elphinstone diverted part of the dakshina, the fund from which the Peshwas had given annual gifts to learned Brahmins, to found the Hindoo College, later the Poona Sanskrit College and in time Deccan College. The great Brahmin families of the city kept their wadas and many of their inams, and some of their sons entered Company service.

By the 1830s Pune was two towns side by side: the old Maratha city on the river and the camp on the plain. It remained the centre of Brahmin learning and political memory in western India, which is why its later history in the century runs so differently from Bombay’s.

In the story

Pune is the Deccan’s most concentrated example of a sovereign city demoted to a provincial one. For a century it had been where Maratha power was negotiated and where the houses of the confederacy came to be confirmed. After 1818 the decisions were made in Bombay and Calcutta and Pune became a garrison and a collectorate. The layered authority of the city, Chhatrapati’s minister, Brahmin establishment, sardars, was reduced to a memory that the city kept and the cantonment ignored.
