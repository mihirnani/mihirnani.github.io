Period 01 · c. 1330–1500

Two Foundations

Delhi’s retreat from the Deccan and the rise of two durable powers – Vijayanagara and the Bahmani sultanate – amid Reddi, Velama and other regional states.

The collection opens with a prologue. In 1327 Muhammad bin Tughluq made Daulatabad a second capital of the Delhi sultanate and, within a decade, lost the south to the officers he had sent there. As Tughluq authority in the peninsula unravelled, two durable powers emerged in a landscape that was already plural: Vijayanagara, founded on the Tungabhadra in the late 1330s by brothers who had served Kampili and perhaps Delhi, amid the disintegration of Hoysala authority; and the Bahmani sultanate, proclaimed in 1347 by a rebel commander and seated at Gulbarga, which descends directly from the revolt of Delhi’s Deccan amirs. Around and between them stood the Reddis of Kondavidu, the Velamas of Rachakonda and the chiefs of the coast. For a century and a half the two great powers fought over the Raichur doab, hired each other’s soldiers, and built capitals – Hampi, Gulbarga, Bidar – that still stand. Their evidence is thinner than the later periods’: chronicles written for courts, inscriptions, and the accounts of travellers. By 1500 the Bahmani state had broken into its provinces and Vijayanagara had changed dynasties once and was about to again; the two Deccans of the next period were already in place.

  • c. 1325–1475 The Reddis and Velamas

    After the Kakatiya fall in 1323, Telugu warrior lineages at Kondavidu, Rajahmundry and Rachakonda ruled coastal Andhra between the two great powers.

    Institution · Other · Bahmani sultanate · Vijayanagara

  • 1327 · Prologue Tughluq moves the capital to Daulatabad

    In 1327 Muhammad bin Tughluq ordered the Delhi court to Devagiri, renamed Daulatabad, to rule the Deccan from inside it; within a decade the experiment had failed.

    Event · Delhi sultanate

  • c. 1335–1378 Madurai and the southern conquest

    Delhi’s southernmost province became an independent sultanate about 1335; a generation later Kumara Kampana conquered it for Vijayanagara, and a Tungabhadra kingdom became an empire of the whole peninsula.

    Event · Vijayanagara · Delhi sultanate · Other

  • c. 1336–1346 The founding of Vijayanagara

    Between about 1336 and 1346 the brothers Harihara and Bukka built a kingdom on the Tungabhadra that became Vijayanagara; how they did so is largely tradition.

    Event · Vijayanagara

  • before 1500 Virupaksha and the sacred centre

    The Virupaksha temple and the older Pampa cult on the Tungabhadra, the sacred ground the Sangamas built their capital beside.

    Place · Vijayanagara

  • 14th–15th century Horses before the Portuguese

    Arab and Persian horses landed at Honavar, Bhatkal and the Konkan ports were the one import both Deccan states could not do without.

    Institution · Vijayanagara · Bahmani sultanate · Other

  • 1347–c. 1430 Gulbarga

    Gulbarga, renamed Ahsanabad, was the first Bahmani capital from 1347 until the court moved to Bidar around 1430; its fort and Jami Masjid survive.

    Place · Bahmani sultanate

  • 3 August 1347 The Bahmani revolt

    In 1347 the rebel amirs of the Deccan made Zafar Khan their sultan as Alauddin Hasan Bahman Shah, ending Delhi’s rule south of the Vindhyas.

    Event · Bahmani sultanate · Delhi sultanate

  • c. 1350–1565 The Raichur doab

    The wedge of land between the Krishna and the Tungabhadra, with its forts at Raichur and Mudgal, was the standing frontier of the Deccan for two centuries.

    Place · Bahmani sultanate · Vijayanagara · Deccan sultanates

  • 14th–15th century Temples and the nayankara

    How Vijayanagara governed: temple endowment as political economy, military tenure for its chiefs, and a long scholarly argument about what kind of state it was.

    Institution · Vijayanagara

  • c. 1350–1565 The doab from below

    The wedge between the Krishna and the Tungabhadra is the oldest ground on the plateau, granite and gneiss with a gold belt through it; the two rivers that bound it were the only lines the rock offered, which is why two centuries of states fought over the wedge and none could hold it.

    Place · Vijayanagara · Bahmani sultanate · Deccan sultanates

  • 1362–1420 The Bahmani–Vijayanagara wars

    Between 1362 and 1420 the Bahmanis and Vijayanagara fought a series of wars over the doab and the coast; the chroniclers’ accounts of them need care.

    Event · Bahmani sultanate · Vijayanagara

  • r. 1397–1422 Firuz Shah Bahmani

    Taj-ud-din Firuz Shah, sultan from 1397 to 1422, opened the Bahmani court to Persian talent, built Firuzabad on the Bhima and, by Firishta’s account, married a Vijayanagara princess.

    Person · Bahmani sultanate · Vijayanagara

  • 1400–1422 Gesudaraz at Gulbarga

    The Chishti saint Sayyid Muhammad Gesudaraz, who settled at Gulbarga in 1400 and died there in 1422, made the Bahmani capital a centre of shrine devotion.

    Person · Bahmani sultanate

  • 15th century Kannada and Telugu at court

    Kumara Vyasa’s Kannada Bharata and Srinatha’s Telugu prabandhas: the two languages of the southern Deccan made literary under royal and Reddi patronage.

    Document · Vijayanagara · Other

  • r. 1424–1446 Deva Raya II

    Deva Raya II, who ruled Vijayanagara from 1424 to 1446, took the state to its first height and recruited Muslim archers and horsemen into its army.

    Person · Vijayanagara · Bahmani sultanate

  • c. 1430–1500 Afaqis and Dakhnis

    The split between immigrant ‘Westerners’ and Deccan-born nobles at the Bahmani court, which turned to massacre in 1446 and 1481 and outlived the dynasty.

    Institution · Bahmani sultanate

  • c. 1430–1619 Bidar

    The second Bahmani capital on its laterite plateau: fort, palaces, the tombs at Ashtur, and the Baridi ministers who inherited it.

    Place · Bahmani sultanate · Deccan sultanates

  • c. 1425–1432 The move to Bidar

    Around 1430 Ahmad Shah I moved the Bahmani capital from Gulbarga to Bidar, renamed Muhammadabad, and rebuilt its fort on a new scale.

    Event · Bahmani sultanate

  • 1435–1541 The Gajapatis of Orissa

    The Suryavamsi kings of Orissa – Kapilendra, Purushottama, Prataparudra – pushed down the Andhra coast for eighty years and were the third power of the Deccan’s first two periods, until Krishnadevaraya drove them back to the Krishna.

    Institution · Other · Vijayanagara · Bahmani sultanate

  • 1442–1444 Abdur Razzaq at Vijayanagara

    The Timurid envoy Abdur Razzaq Samarqandi visited Calicut and Vijayanagara in 1442–44 and described both in his Matla-us-Sadain.

    Document · Vijayanagara · Other

  • 1453–1481 Mahmud Gawan

    The Iranian merchant who became the Bahmani sultanate’s chief minister, reorganised its provinces, took Goa, and was executed on a forged letter in 1481.

    Person · Bahmani sultanate

  • c. 1469–1472 Afanasy Nikitin in the Deccan

    A merchant from Tver who spent about three years in the Bahmani Deccan and left the first Russian account of India, the Journey Beyond Three Seas.

    Document · Bahmani sultanate · Other

  • 1472 The Madrasa of Mahmud Gawan

    A three-storeyed college built in Bidar by the Bahmani vizier on the model of Timurid Central Asia, tiled in blue and green and now half ruined.

    Place · Bahmani sultanate

  • 1482–1528 The Bahmani collapse

    Between the death of Muhammad III in 1482 and the flight of the last nominal sultan in 1528, the Bahmani governors became kings and the minister Qasim Barid kept the crown as a prisoner.

    Event · Bahmani sultanate · Deccan sultanates

  • 1485 The Saluva usurpation

    Saluva Narasimha, a general from Chandragiri, removed the last Sangama king in 1485; within twenty years his own line had been set aside by the Tuluvas.

    Event · Vijayanagara

  • 20 May 1498 The Portuguese at Calicut

    Vasco da Gama anchored off Calicut in May 1498 and met the Zamorin; twelve years later the Portuguese held Goa and a place in the Deccan.

    Event · Portuguese · Other
