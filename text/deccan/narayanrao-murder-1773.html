The murder of Narayanrao and Ramshastri’s verdict

30 August 1773 · Event · Shaniwar Wada, Pune, Maharashtra

The killing of the Peshwa in his own palace on 30 August 1773, the judge who found his uncle responsible, and the council of ministers that ruled for twenty years in the name of a child born after his father’s death.

Narayanrao was seventeen when he became Peshwa in November 1772 on the death of his brother Madhavrao, and he held the office for nine months. His uncle Raghunathrao, who had expected the succession and had already been confined once for intriguing against Madhavrao, was under house arrest in the Shaniwar Wada; the Gardis, the Peshwa’s own regiment of matchlockmen under Sumer Singh, had a grievance over pay and a grudge over their treatment. On 30 August 1773, during the Ganesh festival, a party of Gardis broke into the palace and killed the Peshwa and several of those who tried to shelter him. The tradition that Raghunathrao’s written order to seize his nephew – dharave – was altered by his wife Anandibai to marave, kill him, is old and cannot be proved; the order to seize is not doubted.

Raghunathrao took the seals. What followed was unusual in any state of the age. Ramshastri Prabhune, the nyayadhish or chief judge of the Peshwa’s court, examined the killing, named Raghunathrao as responsible, and pronounced that no penance short of giving up the office could expiate it; he then left Pune rather than serve the man he had condemned. The verdict had no army behind it and could not be executed, but it gave the opposition its ground. Over the following months the leading ministers and sardars – Sakharam Bapu Bokil, Nana Phadnavis, Trimbakrao Pethe, with Holkar and Scindia – formed the council that Maratha historians call the Barbhai, the twelve brethren, in the name of Narayanrao’s widow Gangabai and the child she was carrying. Sawai Madhavrao was born at Purandar fort on 18 April 1774 and invested as Peshwa at forty days old. Raghunathrao, beaten in the field, fled to the Company at Surat and signed away Salsette and Bassein for its support: the war that ended at Wadgaon and Salbai begins here, and so does the regency that governed Pune until 1795.

The episode is a sovereignty judged from within. A state whose power ran through the Chhatrapati’s minister had, in Ramshastri, an officer whose authority came from the dharma he administered rather than the office he served, and who could say that a Peshwa had forfeited the right to be one. The council that acted on his finding ruled for a generation without a Peshwa of age; and the Company, which had backed the loser, learned that Maratha sovereignty was not to be captured by capturing a claimant.
