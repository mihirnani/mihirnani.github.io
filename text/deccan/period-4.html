Period 04 · 1687–1761

The Maratha Century

The empire wins its sieges and loses the war; the Peshwas turn a revenue claim into dominion over half of India.

Aurangzeb took and retook the Marathas’ principal forts and never took the Maratha state. When he died at Ahmadnagar in 1707 the empire’s Deccan was a province in name, and within twelve years the Mughal court had granted the Marathas a legal share of its revenue. The Peshwas of Pune made that share into an instrument of expansion: Bajirao carried Maratha horse to the Narmada, to Delhi and to the Portuguese fortress of Bassein, and the houses of Holkar, Scindia, Gaekwad and Bhonsle took the provinces beyond. The Nizam made his own state at Hyderabad out of the same imperial wreckage, and on the Coromandel coast two European companies began to fight each other with Indian armies. The century ended on a field far from the Deccan, at Panipat.

  • 1690–1698 The siege of Jinji

    An eight-year Mughal siege of the Maratha court in exile at Jinji, in the far south, that ended with the fort taken and the king gone.

    Event · Marathas · Mughal empire

  • c. 1695–1710 Papadu

    A toddy-tapper’s son who built a fort, an army and a fifteen-year bandit kingdom in Mughal Telangana, and sacked Warangal while the empire argued over Aurangzeb’s succession.

    Person · Mughal empire · Other

  • 1675–1761 Tarabai

    Rajaram’s widow, regent from 1700, who held the Maratha war together through Aurangzeb’s last years and founded the rival court at Kolhapur.

    Person · Marathas · Mughal empire

  • 3 March 1707 Aurangzeb dies at Ahmadnagar

    The emperor’s death in his Deccan camp, after twenty-six years in the south, left an empire overextended and a war unwon.

    Event · Mughal empire · Marathas

  • 1708–1749 Chhatrapati and Peshwa

    The dual constitution of the Maratha state: a king at Satara who reigned, and a hereditary Brahmin minister at Pune who governed.

    Institution · Marathas

  • c. 1669–1729 Kanhoji Angre

    The Maratha admiral whose fleet controlled the Konkan coast for thirty years and defied the Company, the Portuguese and the Siddis alike.

    Person · Marathas · East India Company · Portuguese

  • 1715 The Ajnapatra

    A Marathi manual of statecraft written for the young Kolhapur king by Ramchandra Pant Amatya, one of Shivaji’s surviving ministers.

    Document · Marathas

  • 1719 Shahu, Balaji Vishwanath and the sanads of 1719

    Imperial grants of chauth and sardeshmukhi over the Deccan, obtained in Delhi by Shahu’s Peshwa, gave Maratha revenue claims a Mughal legal form.

    Treaty · Marathas · Mughal empire

  • 11 October 1724 Nizam-ul-Mulk establishes the Asaf Jahi state

    Victory at Shakarkheda made the Mughal viceroy of the Deccan an autonomous ruler; the Asaf Jahi state dates from it.

    Event · Hyderabad · Mughal empire · Marathas

  • 28 February 1728 Palkhed, 1728

    Bajirao’s cavalry campaign trapped the Nizam’s army without water and forced him to concede Maratha revenue rights across the Deccan.

    Battle · Marathas · Hyderabad

  • 1700–1740 Bajirao I

    The second Peshwa, who took Maratha arms north of the Narmada and made the Peshwa’s office the real centre of the state.

    Person · Marathas · Mughal empire · Hyderabad

  • February–May 1739 Bassein, 1739

    Chimaji Appa’s capture of the Portuguese fortress of Baçaim ended the Província do Norte and left Bombay surrounded by Maratha territory.

    Battle · Marathas · Portuguese

  • 1728–1761 Holkar, Scindia, Gaekwad, Bhonsle

    The great commanders’ houses that held the Maratha conquests as saranjam and, by mid-century, ruled them as princes.

    Institution · Marathas

  • 1746–1763 The Carnatic wars

    Three Anglo-French wars on the Coromandel coast, fought through Indian claimants, that ended with the Company as the Carnatic’s real power.

    Event · East India Company · Hyderabad · Other

  • 1751–1758, Circars to 1766 Bussy and the French at Hyderabad

    For seven years a French officer and his sepoys were the power behind the Nizam’s throne, paid with the Northern Circars – the first European-managed court in the Deccan, and the model the English perfected.

    Person · Hyderabad · Other · East India Company

  • 3 February 1760 Udgir, 1760

    Sadashivrao Bhau’s methodical destruction of the Nizam’s army brought the Marathas their largest cession of Deccan territory – eleven months before the same commander marched to Panipat.

    Battle · Marathas · Hyderabad

  • c. 1720–1782 Haidar Ali

    A soldier of fortune who by 1761 had displaced the Wodeyar ministers and made Mysore a military state with a sepoy army of its own.

    Person · Mysore · Marathas · Hyderabad · East India Company

  • 14 January 1761 Panipat, 1761

    The Maratha defeat by Ahmad Shah Durrani north of Delhi killed a generation of commanders and ended the Peshwa’s centralised bid to dominate the Mughal empire.

    Battle · Marathas · Mughal empire · Other
