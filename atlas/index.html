Atlas

One map of everything: the entries of The Deccan, 1336–1875, the places of Basalt and Laterite, and the maps of The European Gaze on India, on one coastline.

The peninsula

Circles are Deccan entries, coloured by their principal polity; triangles are Basalt entries, which sit outside the human timeline and are always shown; squares are European maps, one for each map, placed by the region it covers. Move the year to see which Deccan entries and maps belong to it; the rest fade. Hover, tap or tab to a marker and its summary appears in the panel beside the map (below it on a narrow screen); click the marker, or tap it a second time, for its page.

Coastline and rivers from Natural Earth (1:50m land, 1:10m rivers; public domain), clipped to the frame and simplified. The projection is equirectangular, with the east–west scale reduced by cos 16° so that the peninsula keeps its shape. The polity colours are the atlas's own; the Deccan collection groups its entries by period. A sketch for orientation, not a claim about any boundary.
